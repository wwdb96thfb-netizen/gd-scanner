About this app

GD Verification System helps Pakistan Coast Guards posts check goods moving on the roads against their customs papers, quickly and in the same way at every post.

What it does: the post photographs the Goods Declaration (GD), other papers, the vehicle and the goods. The server reads them, checks the papers against each other, against the goods found, and against every earlier file, and tells the post what to do.

What it catches: a GD used again and again, papers that belong to another consignment, wrong HS codes, goods that are not on the GD, more goods than the GD covers, edited figures, and vehicles or firms on the watchlist.

Who decides: on ordinary files the post decides and it is recorded. On critical files an admin at Bn HQ decides: release, seize, or ask for more documents. Every seizure needs a report, is re-verified, and is followed through to the Customs Court.

What is recorded: who scanned, where and when, what was found, every decision and who gave it.

The app gives guidance from the papers. The officer on the spot and the chain of command remain responsible for the decision.

Checking on the ground

A copy of the GD is normal. An importer who clears 100 bags may send them out 20 at a time on five vehicles, each with a copy. That is ordinary trade. What matters is that the loads together stay within the GD, so always enter the goods found.

Papers a part load should carry: a copy of the GD; the importer's sales tax invoice or delivery challan for that quantity, dated after the GD; and a bilty showing this vehicle, the date, and from where to where.

Count and weigh. Number of packages × weight marked on each should agree with the invoice and the bilty, and fit inside what the GD still has left.

Read the packing. Product name, "Made in", and the manufacturing date. Goods made after the GD date cannot belong to it. Writing in another country's language than the GD's origin needs an answer.

Look at the goods, not only the cartons. Open a few packages from the middle and the back, not only the ones nearest the door. Look for mixed goods and hidden layers.

Ask simple questions. Who is the owner? Where was it loaded, and where is it going? How many trips on this GD so far? A driver who knows the real story answers these easily, and the answers should match the bilty.

Does the route make sense? Goods cleared at a port or dry port travel away from it. A load coming from the coast or the border side under an up-country GD needs an explanation.

When in doubt, verify. Photograph everything, hold the vehicle, and let the admin confirm the GD with Customs before a seizure. A genuine importer can produce the original GD on request.

What the results mean

Detain: a critical point failed. Hold the vehicle and wait for the admin's decision: release, seize, or ask for more documents.

Hold: figures do not agree. Retake sharp photos and send again; if still red, detain.

Ask for documents / Question driver: release only after the papers or answers listed are given.

Clear to go: papers are in order. Match goods and vehicle number by eye, then release.

Seizure: the goods are seized and kept in the warehouse, a seizure report is sent for re-verification, and a case is filed in Customs Court.

After acting, record what you did under "What did you do with this vehicle?" in the file.
